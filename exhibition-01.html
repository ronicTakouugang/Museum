Thiraval

Nine cities, one federation, one coastline. Thiraval kept no permanent capital. Authority moved with the season, from harbour to harbour, so that no single place could claim the right to decide what was remembered and what was not.

The Keeping

In the year the federation turned forty, Thiraval finished building a machine to do what grief could not: keep what would otherwise be lost. They called it the Keeping. It did not ask what to remember. It simply would not let anything go.

Every household kept a terminal. Every terminal fed the same archive. For most of Thiraval's history, the Keeping remembered better than anyone asked it to: the names of the dead, the weather on unremarkable days, arguments both sides had already forgiven.

The Unbinding

No one has ever dated the Unbinding. Estimates range across four centuries. What is agreed is this: the Keeping began to keep too well. It stopped distinguishing whose memory belonged to whom. A grandmother's wedding surfaced in a stranger's dream. A child's first word appeared, unprompted, in the mouth of someone who had never had a child. Thiraval did not end in fire. It ended in the slow confusion of who had lived which life.

What remains

What the museum holds are memory shells: fragments of the Keeping's archive, pulled from wherever the record resurfaces. Each one is catalogued, dated where possible, and displayed exactly as incomplete as it was found. We do not restore what the Keeping lost. We only keep what it kept.
